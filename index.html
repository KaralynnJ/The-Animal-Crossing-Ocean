The Deep Ocean

By Karalynn Jones

THE SUNLIGHT ZONE
The brightest, warmest and most populated layer of the ocean, only 200m deep.
THE TWILIGHT ZONE
This layer of the ocean stretches between 200m and 1000m. A bit of sun can still get here.
Humans swim easily here.
Blowfish carry a deadly poison and are one of the most toxic creature on earth. On top of that, it can inflate itself as a defense mechanism.
Seahorses wrap their tail around seaweed or coral so they don't float away in the current.
Butterfly fish swim by flapping their fins, making them look like butterflies. They prefer to swim in schools.
Clownfish produce a special coating that prevents the sting of sea anemones, allowing them to live in their feelers.
Most reefs need sunlight to photosynthesize, so they sit around 20m.
The surgeonfish is rather timid and will hise between rocks or in coral to sleep.
Unless it's the Great Barrier Reef, which has its shallows at 35m.
Thye long fins are very pretty, but they're hiding poisonous spikes. They're a type of lionfish.
Despite being known as bottom-feeders, sturgeon rarely go beyond 60m deep. They are best known for caviar.
The Giant Trevally sometimes uses other predators to distract their prey, and can jump out of the water to catch sea birds. It stays around 80m.
The mahi-mahi is sometimes called the dolphinfish, though they aren't dolphins. They live around 85m.
Napoleonfish, also called the humphead wrasse, are all born female. As they mature, some turn into males and become more blue.
Alexey Molchanoc has the deepest equipment-free freediving record at 102m.
The Thick-Billed Murre can dive to a depth of about 210m, the deepest of any flighted bird.
The deepest scuba dive is 332m, set by Ahmed Gabr.
Sea butterflies are a type of sea snail that swim by flapping their winglike arms. They float up to the surface at night to feed and go down to ~400m during the day to hide from predators.
Emperor penguins dive the deepest, reaching a max of 565m. They can stay underwater for up to 30 minutes at a time.
Blue marlins usually swim in the top 100m but can dive as deep as 600m to feed.
Bluefin tuna are one of the deepest diving tuna, reaching depths of ~800m.
When salmon are in the ocean, they've been found at depths over 1000m. They return to fresh waters to spawn.
